# Loaded-working-set failure amendment

**Historical specification:** the loading, between-rep rests and warm-up flow below are superseded by [App 7.20](SESSION-USABILITY-REVIEW.md). The miss/form-failure requirement remains.

**Historical 7.16 record.** Its one-set Olympic allocation and three-stage introduction are superseded by [the 7.17 set-dose review](SET-DOSE-REVIEW.md). Other endpoint/scheduling constraints continue unless explicitly amended there.

User clarification, September 21, 2026: **loaded working sets only; Olympic endpoint is either a missed attempt or a technically invalid rep.** This later request supersedes contradictory Olympic endpoint, workload and test instructions in the original PDF. The PDF itself remains unchanged. App 7.16 implements the amendment separately from source prescriptions and historic records.

## Prescription

- Every retained conventional working set remains strict-form failure. Every retained Olympic/derivative exercise has **one fixed-load working set**, with **15-second resets** after valid reps. The first miss or grade-C rep ends it immediately. A CJ rep requires a valid clean AND jerk; either component failing ends the set. No retries, forced reps, within-set load changes or extra sets. Pain/unsafe/voluntary stops are incomplete, not manufactured failure outcomes.
- Warm-ups, technical preparation, athletics, aerobic activity and mobility remain outside the new requirement. Existing general/local ramps and all scheduled recovery activities remain runnable and timed. Technical restrictions omit affected loaded work instead of disguising submaximal work as preparation.
- Ordinary order remains Mon B / Tue C / Thu A / Fri D. Foundation/Build retain nine Olympic failure sets weekly; Realization retains eight because the pull is absent. This replaces 37/36/31 quality sets, respectively; these counts are not claims of equivalent dose or adaptation.
- Valid-rep load-selection windows: Foundation **2–4**, Build **1–3**, Realization **1–2**; pulls **3–5**. The app does not end a set at the window. It allocates the upper target plus one terminal attempt for the initial time budget and adds actual attempts/resets as necessary.
- First-exposure estimates: **80/85/88%** of the appropriate reference in Foundation/Build/Realization. Hang snatch uses ten percentage points less; pulls ten more. Rack jerk without an assessed RJ uses **65% CJ**. Use only with secure preparation; lower or defer when needed. Round down to the selected plate increment.
- Next-exposure progression: compare the same exercise, slot, setup, endpoint and rep window. Repeat the previous work load; reduce about **5–10%** below the valid-rep window. Increase one selected plate increment only after **two comparable normal exposures** reach the top of the window before their terminal attempt, at the same load, with normal subsequent recovery. A zero-valid-rep set triggers review and does not earn progression. Introduction/checkpoints/Realization/taper hold increases.
- Effort 1–10 is descriptive, not a stopping cap. Five minutes of recovery precedes the next loaded exercise, followed by its local ramp; loading/logging may share recovery. Add recovery when needed. No timer can certify an endpoint.
- Adoption starts conventional volume at one set per retained exercise. Complete green weeks with next-session follow-ups progress to the source intermediate dose and then the established dose. At the established dose, two full green weeks at stable workload are required before optional additions. Older or mixed-policy weeks cannot qualify. Existing source Olympic heavy/extra-attempt, pause-jerk and bounded assessment options are superseded; load progression follows valid reps.
- Amber/global fatigue, targeted/reset reductions and sport later the same day omit Olympic failure work. Local issues omit affected rows. Verification/restore hold Olympic load increases. The usual alcohol, bench-protection and actual **48-hour bench-spacing** checks continue. A limited event later does not erase an otherwise normal exposure's progression eligibility.
- Week 12: Monday one snatch and one CJ failure set plus low-rep bench; Tuesday/Thursday no loaded work; Friday one fixed-load failure benchmark per lift; moderate bench afterward. This is **not** a three-attempt mock meet or a competition total. Week 13: no Olympic work sets, one set per retained conventional exercise. Recovery reduces volume rather than pretending submaximal working sets meet the new endpoint.

All exact new numbers above are practical inferences for an experimental, preference-constrained routine. The reviewed literature does not determine them or establish this routine as optimal/equivalent for the user's priorities.

## Evidence and limits

- **General resistance training:** Vieira et al. (2021) pooled 13 studies. Overall strength and power comparisons did not establish a failure advantage; volume-matched hypertrophy comparisons also did not establish one. Some non-volume-matched comparisons favored non-failure for strength/power. These are not direct trials of the proposed Olympic routine. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33555822/)
- **Proximity and hypertrophy:** Robinson et al. (2024) found an association between closer estimated proximity to failure and greater hypertrophy, while strength gains were similar across a range of estimated reserve repetitions. The analysis was exploratory and RIR was estimated. It does not establish that mandatory misses in snatches or jerks maximize either outcome. [Author institution record and abstract](https://rke.abertay.ac.uk/en/publications/exploring-the-dose-response-relationship-between-estimated-resist/)
- **Power and fatigue tradeoff:** Pareja-Blanco et al. studied 22 young men during eight weeks of squat training. A 20% velocity-loss condition produced greater jump improvement than a 40% condition, with similar squat-strength gains and fewer repetitions; some hypertrophy outcomes favored the higher-loss condition. Velocity loss is not synonymous with failure, and this was squat training, not a snatch/clean trial. Applying it to the proposed redesign is an inference. [Trial abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27038416/)
- **Weightlifting technique:** Hardee et al. studied ten male recreational lifters performing three sets of six power cleans at 80% 1RM with different rests between repetitions. Cluster configurations better maintained aspects of bar displacement than continuous sets. This is acute evidence about power-clean technique, not evidence of long-term superiority or a quantified injury rate from failure training. [Author manuscript in institutional repository](https://libres.uncg.edu/ir/asu/f/Zwetsloot_2013_Effect_cluster.pdf.X.pdf)
- **Acute recovery cost:** Vieira et al.'s acute-fatigue review included 20 studies, 12 in meta-analysis. Failure conditions produced greater acute fatigue and impairment in measured mechanical performance on average. This supports reassessing total dose and recovery; it does not determine a universal 48- or 72-hour recovery prescription for an individual. [Author institution abstract](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/effects-of-resistance-training-to-muscle-failure-on-acute-fatigue/)

The reviewed evidence does not establish an all-to-failure Olympic regimen as optimal or equivalent to the existing quality-focused work. That is an evidence limit, not proof that every possible failure-based program is ineffective. A redesign could be an explicitly preference-constrained experiment, with uncertainty stated; that interpretation must not be confused with the previous demand for no sacrifice in optimality.

## Implementation and compatibility

`src/failure-policy.js` owns the amendment and next-load logic. New Olympic rows retain their Olympic exercise identity, ramp, and per-attempt clean/jerk outcomes while carrying `endpointPolicy: first-invalid-v1`. Source quality rows keep their original semantics. This avoids routing snatches through conventional accessory preparation.

`training.js` closes a new Olympic set at its actual endpoint, distinguishes valid reps from attempts, requires recovery evidence for increases, omits restricted active work and does not penalize the expected terminal attempt through the old 90% good-rep trigger. Conventional and Olympic familiar-load rep loss remain reviewable.

`timeline.js` and `pacing.js` use a frozen initial budget and a variable queue. Early terminal attempts remove unused reps/rests; valid reps beyond the budget extend the queue. Reload, undo and offline recovery preserve the actual journal. `planner-feed.js` includes endpoint/reset/recovery fields in prescription signatures so old quality-session duration samples cannot calibrate the new protocol. Planner's existing feed consumer accepts the amended durations and special-week sessions without a schema change.

Existing unstarted work adopts without waiting for a new week. A session already active keeps its frozen prescription until finished or ended, then upcoming work adopts. No historical outcome, date or active prescription is relabeled. Storage validates the new endpoint contract, including fixed load and no attempts after the first invalid rep. Explicit source fixtures remain available for regression testing the old program and frozen histories.

## Verification

The amendment has dedicated automated coverage in `tests/failure-policy.test.js` and `tests/failure-browser.mjs`, alongside the existing source, journal, calendar, timer, migration and deployment suites. Acceptance checks include all 52 weeks, each readiness/event/recovery context, technique restrictions, missed clean/jerk, grade-C endpoints, zero valid reps, a voluntary/safety stop, extra valid reps beyond the time budget, constant-load enforcement, early completion, undo/offline reload, old active-session migration, optional-dose gates, matching Planner signatures and a full 196-session year with both bench exposures each week.

Passing these checks demonstrates tested app behavior; it does not prove biological optimality or the absence of every possible bug. Publication requires the complete audit and exact live-asset verification.
