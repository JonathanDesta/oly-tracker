# Guided training changes · App 7.23

**Week or Settings → Add or adjust training** is the starting point for optional training. The original program and subsequent user amendments still determine training doses. This update replaces the common path through the all-purpose change form with goal-specific plans.

## User flow

- **Jumps & short runs:** shows the current dose, one concrete next change, the affected dates, whole-day time and added time. A collapsed roadmap explains the initial progression. Later options cover a second day, flying runs and cuts; they are alternatives, not a checklist to complete together.
- **Easy cardio:** starts at two 20-minute sessions. The current three-day lifting calendar places these on Tuesday and Saturday. It progresses by five minutes until both sessions reach 30 minutes, then by ten weekly minutes distributed as walks. The preview uses actual saved dates, including moved days.
- **Lifting sets:** choose from exercises actually present on each day, then add or remove one weekly set. The confirmation names the exercise, day and before/after set count. Specialized substitutions remain available separately.
- **Targeted stretching:** links to the existing restriction selection. Its preparation, stretching and active checks remain runnable on the appropriate day tabs.

The landing screen explains the current lifting build-up and the two reviewed weeks at the full workload. It links directly to Weekly review or History instead of asking users to interpret “entry,” “stage,” “primary,” or generic eligibility checkboxes. The optional-change confirmation asks only for the relevant performance/recovery confirmation, plus the full-cycle-at-150 confirmation when applicable.

Saving a change opens its day tab. The prescribed preparation, work, rest and transition timers are generated by the existing workout engine. Nothing automatically advances to a harder athletic dose. Keeping the current dose needs no action; reduce/pause controls are inside each plan. A precise undo can restore the last athletic change's saved before-state. Existing session records remain unchanged.

## Progression and safeguards

The source's initial athletic doses remain: 2 × 3 jumps and 3 × 10 m at 85–90%; then 3 × 3 jumps; then 3 × 15 m; then 90–95% effort; then alternating jump and running increments. Six jumps is familiarization, not a permanent target. The review points and later options are not mandatory quotas or scientifically established ceilings.

The guided flow counts completed successful sessions at the changed dose, with a normal next-lifting-session follow-up. It excludes incomplete sessions, omitted work, bad quality, missing recovery checks, shortened doses and unrelated sessions. A change to the second day counts that day. A running-variation change counts the varied sessions. Two reviewed weeks are tracked after an aerobic change. An unfinished observation is shown before another guided optional change or set addition.

The shared prescription mutation powers both preview and save. Existing `applyChange` checks remain authoritative at save time; the guided path also rechecks its observations and the user's confirmation. Merely opening a plan or preview does not persist anything. Day estimates come from the same calendar, prescription and fixed timer code used by workouts, including alternating running variations. Actual readiness and interruptions may still adjust the day.

Workout starts, dates, repeats and full-plan overrides remain user-controlled under the 7.22 amendment. This update does not reintroduce consecutive-day or workout-order locks.

## Evidence and inference

The priority order remains Olympic lifting, hypertrophy, athleticism, then health-only aerobic expansion. A justified higher-priority change comes first, but starting athletics does not require exhausting lifting additions or waiting for hypertrophy to stall (source pp.21–22,29).

[Sprint-development review, Haugen et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31754845/) supports individualized progression, specificity and appropriate recovery while documenting substantial gaps between research populations and elite practice. It does not validate this app's exact sequence.

[Jump-training scoping review, Ramirez-Campillo et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10457889/) describes substantial variation and reporting gaps in training variables. It does not establish six jumps as a development minimum or identify a unique best progression for a weightlifter doing loaded sets to failure.

[Concurrent-training meta-analysis, Schumann et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8891239/) found no overall impairment of maximal strength or whole-muscle hypertrophy, but an explosive-strength concern, particularly with same-session aerobic and strength training. These findings do not directly test this jump/sprint module or guarantee zero interference. Keeping formal cardio on recovery days and athletics after priority lifts in one visit retains the user's scheduling compromise.

The exact starting doses, two-session/two-week review windows, ordering and thresholds are practical inferences from the source program and user constraints. The interface labels them as reviewable choices, not a proven personal optimum.

## Verification

`tests/additions.test.js` covers preview/save/timer agreement across the introductory progression, observation accounting, second-day prerequisites, variation replacements and efforts, cardio progression, paused work, phase holds and required confirmations. `tests/additions-browser.mjs` exercises the actual mobile UI in Chromium and WebKit, including persistence, time parity, reduction, navigation, held weeks and horizontal overflow. The complete existing audit remains required before release.

The release audit also exposed an alarm resume race: a queued pause event from an old audio track could mark the replacement track interrupted even while it was playing. The handler now checks the current media's paused state. A regression test covers that event ordering. The browser alarm check distinguishes a continuing delayed track from the existing immediate-alarm recovery on return; it verifies that recovery does not restart the silent countdown. Physical iPhone background/lock-screen reliability remains subject to the platform limitations already shown in Settings.
