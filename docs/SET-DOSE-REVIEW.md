# Set-dose review · App 7.17 · September 21, 2026

The user requested the best-supported exercise and muscle set counts under the existing constraints, and clarified that they are **starting/restarting, with historically good recovery**. This review replaces 7.16’s permanent one-set Olympic allocation. It does not claim that a software audit can identify an individual physiological maximum. The prescription is an explicit starting allocation plus a usable dose-adjustment process; deciding the eventual individual dose requires training observations.

## Evidence and decision boundaries

- **Weekly volume:** Pelland et al., 67 studies, found a positive dose response with diminishing returns. Fractional accounting fitted better than counting every indirect set fully or ignoring it. The authors describe it as a heuristic, identify limited failure-specific moderator data, and caution that muscle-specific results do not establish the cost of maximizing every muscle simultaneously in a whole-body program. Most included effects used some failure definition, so failure does not justify automatically dividing conventional set recommendations by a large factor. [Published paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w), [full paper](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf).
- **Multiple sets:** the 2026 ACSM overview supports 2–3 sets for strength and higher weekly muscle volume for hypertrophy. These are general findings, not an Olympic-to-technical-failure prescription or a rule that all muscles must receive an identical dose. They support moving the squat and bench reference from one to two sets per exposure. [ACSM overview](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/).
- **Olympic lifting:** a ten-week trial in experienced junior weightlifters favored moderate rather than the lowest/highest tested volume for some strength outcomes. It does not establish how many technical-failure clusters replace ordinary singles/doubles. Therefore, the revised 15-set allocation is an extrapolation with direct outcome review, not a validated conversion. [González-Badillo et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16095427/).
- **Failure:** conventional failure adds acute fatigue compared with stopping earlier; this supports a gradual restart and recovery monitoring, not an exact set-reduction ratio. [Vieira et al., acute fatigue review](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/effects-of-resistance-training-to-muscle-failure-on-acute-fatigue/). A single-set failure-versus-RIR trial has no multi-set comparator and cannot establish single-set optimality. [Hermann et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40249908/).
- **Per-session dose:** Remmert et al.’s inspected version is a preprint. Its modeled point of undetectable superiority is not a physiological ceiling. It is not used to impose an exact per-session cap here. [Primary preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/download/537/1148/1071).

The exact allocation, four-stage restart, two-week initial trial window and review landmarks below are practical inferences. Evidence supports their direction more strongly than their precise numbers. No claim is made that long sessions, mandatory Olympic failure, or alcohol exposure preserve unrestricted optimality.

## Preserved constraints

Weightlifting performance remains first, followed by hypertrophy (upper chest, side delts and traps emphasized), athleticism and longevity. Every loaded work set retains the user's failure endpoint; Olympic sets end at their first missed or technically invalid rep. Preparation, athletics, cardio and mobility keep their own endpoints. The default remains one gym visit on Monday B, Tuesday C, Thursday A and Friday D. There are two flat barbell bench exposures with at least 48 actual hours between exposures. No glute isolation is introduced. Exercise selection, safe barbell endpoints, equipment alternatives, actual drinking/event rules, readiness restrictions, athletic introduction, warm-ups and mobility stay applicable.

## Restart and ordinary established allocation

Each complete green week can advance one stage, only with normal next-session follow-ups. Incomplete, mixed-version or abnormal work cannot earn advancement. Historically good recovery supports trying this progression; it cannot establish current tolerance in advance. Two full green weeks at the established comparable workload are then required before discretionary additions. Phase changes or reductions can interrupt that window. Repeating a weekly review does not manufacture another week.

## Muscle accounting at the established reference

Direct conventional sets count as 1 and indirect sets as 0.5 for the displayed estimate. This is a transparent approximation, not a biological measurement. Exercise-specific allocation is itself inferred: rows count directly for mid/upper back and indirectly for lats, rear delts and elbow flexors; pulldowns count directly for lats and indirectly for mid/upper back and elbow flexors. ## Adjustment process across every exercise

The established reference is not the final answer for every muscle. Lower doses for non-priority regions allocate recovery and time to weightlifting and the stated physique priorities. Calling them individually maximal would be unsupported. After the initial stable-dose review, the app supports one added weekly set on any existing Olympic row, bench, either squat or any accessory. Choose the day/exercise, record baseline and purpose, and hold other dose additions. Retention requires two distinct normal weeks with completed target work and normal subsequent recovery; physique assessment still needs a full cycle. The two-week check mainly establishes early tolerance. An improving lift does not prove maximal hypertrophy.

Review landmarks, not ceilings or automatic targets:

1. Address a demonstrated weightlifting/strength need first: additional specific Olympic practice or squat support. Compare valid reps, video, technical consistency and the next priority session. There is no universal upper bound of one or two sets.
2. For the physique priorities, consider incline toward 10–12 direct weekly sets, laterals toward 10–12 and shrugs toward 6, one weekly set at a time. Give traps a trial without waiting to exhaust chest/delt additions.
3. Review back regions toward 8–12 fractional sets, rear delts toward 6–8 and arms toward 8–12 including indirect work. These ranges guide a trial, not a declaration that lower/higher doses cannot be optimal for this individual.
4. Review quads toward 10–12 conventional sets, hamstrings/calves toward 6–10 direct and abs toward 4–6. No glute-isolation exception is created. Do not force every target within one cycle or raise everything simultaneously.

“Remove one weekly work set after review” can pause a matching addition or reduce an established exercise, down to one retained set. Full omission remains available for unsafe/unready work. Restoring a removed set follows the controlled addition process. Existing monitoring flags (>20% first-set rep loss twice, >5% comparable Olympic load loss, altered positions) and immediate safety rules act before a scheduled trial review. Reduced/verification work, taper and pivot override reference counts. Week 11 D has one conventional set per retained exercise; week 12 has four total Olympic sets and two total bench sets; week 13 has no Olympic work and 28 conventional sets before individual restrictions. Normal Realization has 14 Olympic sets because pulls are absent.

## Runner, timing and compatibility

Each Olympic set ends independently at its first miss/invalid rep. The same planned load remains fixed; at least five minutes separates prescribed sets. A zero-valid-rep set or the same recorded material fault in two sets ends the exercise. Pain/unsafe symptoms end the session. The runner tracks set number and valid reps separately; a nominal rep budget cannot end a set. Early/late endpoints rebuild only the remaining pacing sequence, with no replacement sets. Load increases require every prescribed set in two comparable normal exposures to reach the top of the rep window. First-set monitoring compares first sets, not the combined reps of a multi-set exercise.

The timer includes every set, rest, ramp, transition and existing miscellaneous allowance. With default moderate traffic, no optional modules or mobility, the ordinary Foundation reference estimates approximately **253/86/75/260 minutes** for Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri. These are planning budgets, not evidence that four-hour sessions are optimal, and no shorter clock is obtained by secretly removing warm-ups or rest. Actual performance loss, missed work or inability to fit a visit warrants adjusting dose/order; this release preserves the approved weekdays and one-visit default rather than assuming new school-year availability. Planner receives the actual new durations and changed prescription signatures; old time observations cannot silently calibrate a different dose.

App 7.16 active sessions and historic rows keep their original endpoint version and prescription. The new dose is queued until an active session ends; new unstarted work migrates to the reviewed allocation at its existing introduction stage. Stable-dose observations reset, and mixed-version weeks cannot earn advancement. Source-profile fixtures continue to test the unamended PDF independently. The PDF itself is unchanged.
