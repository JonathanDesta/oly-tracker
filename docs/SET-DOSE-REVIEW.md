# Whole-week recommendation · App 7.19 · September 21, 2026

**Selected routine: Monday, Wednesday and Friday, one visit per day. Both competition lifts start each visit. The established normal-week allocation is 12 Olympic and 56 conventional working sets.** This is the strongest current recommendation I can justify for the combined goals and constraints, replacing the separate muscle-by-muscle accumulation in 7.18.

This decision is an evidence-informed prescription, not an experimentally identified personal optimum. The evidence supports the components and the reasoning below; it cannot prove equal or superior outcomes for this exact athlete. That uncertainty is a reason to use a coherent starting prescription and defined response checks, not to leave the allocation unfinished.

## Requirements and priority order

Olympic performance comes first; hypertrophy follows, emphasizing upper chest, side delts and traps; athleticism and longevity follow. The user is restarting, with historically good recovery. Every **loaded working set** retains the requested failure endpoint. Olympic sets finish at the first miss or technically invalid rep; conventional work finishes at strict-form failure with a safe endpoint. Preparation, athletics, cardio and mobility are outside that failure constraint. There are no forced reps, drop sets or deliberate collapsed squat/bench reps.

Flat barbell bench remains twice weekly, one low-rep exposure, at least 48 actual hours apart. No glute isolation. One visit per day avoids a second gym trip/change. Normal weeks avoid weekend lifting; readiness rules still govern Friday after Thursday drinking and Monday after weekend drinking. Three days replaces the previous four-day recommendation; four lifting days was not an independently required user constraint. The original PDF is unchanged. Later requests to adapt the regimen and impose failure govern this amendment.

The three-day plan is not selected simply because it is shorter. It improves spacing without reducing full-lift frequency. The former hang-snatch, rack-jerk and pull blocks consume failure/recovery capacity and extra preparation. Without a demonstrated technical limitation requiring one, allocate that capacity to the competition lifts. A newly demonstrated limitation could justify a replacement at reassessment; it does not automatically justify additional failure sets.

Monday starts with CJ, Wednesday/Friday with snatch, so CJ also gets a fresh first position. Olympic work precedes all assistance. Squats and bench lead the conventional blocks; priority incline/lateral work follows. Most horizontal/vertical pulling is concentrated Wednesday after the lifts, using supported rows to limit additional spinal fatigue. Wrist work finishes Wednesday/Friday. When athletics is enabled, it runs **after Olympic work but before assistance**, within the same visit, with its own warm-up and quality limits.

## Evidence used, and what is inferred

- [Pelland et al., 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w) pooled 67 studies and supports increasing volume with diminishing returns; fractional indirect-set accounting fitted their data better than direct-only or total-set counting. Frequency had clearer associations with strength than hypertrophy. This supports preserving specific lift frequency and considering overlap. It does **not** supply a universal 56-set whole-body optimum or an exact credit for every exercise.
- [ACSM's 2026 overview](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/) supports multiple sets and relatively higher volume for hypertrophy. Its general guidance is not a requirement for ten isolated failure sets for every muscle in a weightlifting-first routine. Chest has ten direct sets here; priority side delts receive eight; traps receive four direct sets plus substantial Olympic exposure without invented numerical conversion.
- [Vieira et al., acute-fatigue review](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/effects-of-resistance-training-to-muscle-failure-on-acute-fatigue/) supports a greater fatigue cost from failure training. [Failure-versus-nonfailure outcomes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33555822/) do not establish failure as superior for strength or power. The user's failure requirement remains; lower combined volume and recovery days are the practical response. There is no scientifically justified constant for converting conventional weightlifting sets into repeated technical-failure sets.
- [González-Badillo et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16095427/) compared three volumes in experienced junior weightlifters and favored moderate over the tested high/low volumes for some outcomes. This supports not treating maximal tolerable work as maximal benefit; it did not test this failure protocol. [Hardee et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23121475/) concerns acute rest configurations, not proof of this long-term allocation.
- [Zhang et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39903375/) found supersets can save time with similar average adaptations, but greater internal load/RPE and variation by pairing. They are not the default here: Olympic/squat/bench quality is prioritized, every working set reaches failure, and occupying multiple stations in a moderately busy gym is not assumed. The timer does not silently overlap exercises or remove prescribed recovery.
- Regional selection uses longitudinal evidence where available: [squat depth/adductors](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31230110/), [seated versus prone leg curls](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7969179/), [standing versus seated calves](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38156065/), and [overhead triceps extensions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/). These support exercise coverage, not the exact counts chosen here. No extra failure hinge is needed merely to include hamstrings; no separate seated-calf block is needed merely to include soleus.
- [Szymanski et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15320673/) supports specific wrist/forearm-strength benefits from direct training in adolescent baseball players. It does not identify optimal forearm hypertrophy volume. Two wrist-flexion and two wrist-extension sets, plus one hammer-curl set and pulling exposure, are deliberate allocations to otherwise under-addressed functions, with a modest time/fatigue cost.

The selected counts are programming judgments made together: retain substantial priority-region work, supply complementary lower-dose regional work, and cap the cumulative failure burden. A fourth priority-region set is not automatically more valuable than a fresh next Olympic session. Conversely, merely feeling recovered does not prove there is no benefit from additional work. The response review below makes that uncertainty actionable.

## Progression and review

Restart stages prescribe **31 → 41 → 46 → 56 conventional sets** and **6 → 6 → 9 → 12 Olympic sets**. They are not four mandatory consecutive calendar weeks: each stage needs a complete green week plus normal subsequent-session follow-ups. Checkpoints, Realization, taper and pivot do not earn an increase. An ordinary successful introduction reaches stage 4 in week 5 because week 4 is a checkpoint. Good historical recovery justifies the intended progression; it does not skip re-entry observation.

At the established allocation, hold the dose for two stable green weeks before a single-variable trial. Use comparable first-set reps, successful Olympic quality before the endpoint, and the following session's performance. The app flags a greater-than-20% familiar-load first-set loss on two exposures. Review physique photographs/circumferences over a full cycle, not a two-week soreness test. Add at most one weekly set to one existing exercise when the expected benefit justifies it; reverse a trial without useful benefit or with a cost to Olympic performance. The chosen baseline is fully prescribed now, not deferred to optional additions.

Week 11 reduces each retained conventional exercise and each competition lift to one set per visit. Week 12 retains Monday failure work, Friday fixed-load benchmarks, and moderate bench after the benchmark. Week 13 has no Olympic failure work and one set per conventional exercise across the three days. Readiness, pain, protection, alcohol and actual bench-spacing rules can reduce or defer any prescribed work.

Existing started weeks retain their saved four-day order until the weekly review boundary. Then the new allocation starts at no higher than introduction stage 2, with stable-dose observations reset and old optional trials paused for review. Active workouts and historical prescriptions remain unchanged. Empty weeks adopt immediately. This prevents an update from moving partially completed work or replaying completed sessions.
