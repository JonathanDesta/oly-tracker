# Complete regional allocation · App 7.18 · September 21, 2026

This supersedes the incomplete 7.17 allocation. The user requested a decision for every relevant muscle group and exercise, including forearms, using research and reasonable inference. The previous allocation left several lower doses as placeholders for future optional additions and did not justify the absence of direct forearm work. That was incomplete.

This release makes the exercise allocation and 34 regional decisions explicit. It is my current programming judgment under the user's priorities, not an empirically identified individual optimum. More work for every muscle is not automatically better for the combined goals. No study establishes that this whole program, repeated Olympic technical-failure sets, or its resulting five-hour B/D visits maximize the user's outcomes. A software audit cannot establish those claims.

## Constraints used

Starting/restarting with historically good recovery; Olympic performance first, then hypertrophy emphasizing upper chest/side delts/traps, then athleticism and longevity. Loaded working sets retain the user's failure endpoints. Two flat-barbell bench exposures, one low-rep, retain actual ≥48-hour spacing. No glute isolation. The approved Mon B / Tue C / Thu A / Fri D calendar, one-visit default, readiness/alcohol overrides and optional-module gates remain. Original PDF content is unchanged; later user amendments govern conflicts.

## Evidence and inference

- [Pelland et al., online 2025 / issue 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w): increasing conventional weekly volume is associated with greater gains and diminishing returns. Fractional indirect-set accounting fits the pooled data better than simple alternatives. This does not establish a universal plateau, per-muscle minimum, optimal whole-body total, or exact 0.5 credit for every exercise in this user.
- [ACSM 2026 overview](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/): multiple sets and higher weekly volume support strength/hypertrophy. General guidance around ten muscle-building sets is a population landmark, not proof that every muscle needs ten isolated failure sets in a weightlifting-first program.
- [Robinson et al. 2024](https://rke.abertay.ac.uk/en/publications/exploring-the-dose-response-relationship-between-estimated-resist/) supports the relevance of proximity to failure for hypertrophy. [Vieira et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33555822/) does not establish failure as universally superior. Technical failure in Olympic lifting does not establish momentary muscular failure of every contributing muscle.
- [Szymanski et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15320673/): 43 adolescent baseball players trained for 12 weeks; adding wrist/forearm exercises improved most specific wrist/forearm strength tests more than the general program, without a between-group advantage in grip. This was not a forearm hypertrophy dose-response trial. Four wrist-flexion sets, four wrist-extension sets and two hammer-curl sets are explicit conservative inferences, not numbers extracted from that study. Neutral-grip elbow flexion is selected for anatomical coverage of brachialis/brachioradialis, not from acute EMG as proof of superior growth.
- [Kubo et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31230110/): full-depth squatting grew adductors/gluteus maximus, while rectus femoris and hamstrings did not significantly grow in that experiment. This supports retaining leg extensions and curls and recognizing squat adductor exposure. It does not validate an exact adductor-set conversion.
- [Maeo et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7969179/): seated leg curls produced greater biarticular hamstring growth than prone curls; both trained the short biceps-femoris head. This supports seated curls as efficient regional coverage; six weekly failure sets are our inference, not the study's tested prescription.
- [Kinoshita et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38156065/): knee-extended calf training produced more gastrocnemius growth than seated training, with similar soleus growth. Standing work therefore covers both regions without automatically requiring a second calf exercise. The study does not prove six sets optimal.
- [Maeo et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/): overhead cable extensions produced more triceps growth than the neutral-arm comparison, supporting retention of direct overhead work alongside presses. Two direct sets plus press overlap is a constrained allocation, not demonstrated maximal long-head growth.
- [González-Badillo et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16095427/) studied differing volumes in junior weightlifters; [Hardee et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23121475/) studied acute power-clean rest configurations. Neither supports a precise conversion from conventional weightlifting sets to the user's repeated technical-failure sets. The retained 15-set Olympic allocation is an uncertain inference, reviewed by quality and recovery.

The selected distribution puts substantial work into the priority regions and supplies direct work where compounds leave a meaningful coverage gap. Moderate allocations for other regions limit additional fatigue/time. Reasons for zero isolation are recorded below instead of implying that stabilization is maximal hypertrophy training. Failure, body size, training age and historical recovery cannot be converted into an exact set multiplier from current evidence.

## Regional accounting and decisions

Direct conventional sets count as 1, indirect sets as 0.5. These are rough workload estimates, not measured growth stimuli. Olympic sets receive no numeric hypertrophy credit. Regional rows overlap (e.g. calf heads, elbow flexors and upper chest) and must not be summed into total program volume. An exercise can train multiple groups, but doing two sets does not make it six performed sets. Equipment substitutions alter coverage; a seated-calf fallback removes the numeric gastrocnemius credit, without claiming no possible gastrocnemius stimulus.

- **Chest (all regions):** 12 direct sets: four flat-bench sets retain the required strength exposures; eight incline sets prioritize the upper chest without adding another press.
- **Upper chest (regional subset):** Eight incline sets, plus uncounted flat-bench contribution. Keep four incline sets per exposure; adding more presses has a recovery/time cost for the priority lifts.
- **Front delts:** Zero isolation: 12 pressing sets give six fractional sets, with additional jerks. Another overhead-press block duplicates an already loaded region.
- **Side delts:** Ten direct lateral-raise sets, five per exposure. This is a physique priority and gets a larger direct allocation.
- **Rear delts:** Four reverse-pec-deck sets plus six rows (seven fractional sets). Dedicated work covers rear delts without assuming every row fully trains them.
- **Upper traps:** Six direct shrug sets, three per exposure, plus unconverted Olympic work. Traps receive an explicit priority dose even if straps are used.
- **Mid/upper back:** Six supported-row sets plus four pulldowns (eight fractional sets). Support limits extra spinal fatigue.
- **Lats:** Four pulldown sets plus six rows (seven fractional sets). Full overhead reach provides the vertical-pull exposure; the row contribution depends on technique.
- **Biceps / elbow flexors:** Two supinated-curl and two hammer-curl sets plus ten pulling sets (nine fractional sets). Two grips provide complementary elbow-flexor work.
- **Brachialis (overlapping elbow-flexor region):** Two hammer-curl sets counted directly, with additional supinated curling/pulling. This overlaps the elbow-flexor total; do not add the two totals.
- **Brachioradialis (forearm elbow flexor):** Two neutral-grip hammer-curl sets, one per exposure, plus unconverted pulling/curling. Neutral-grip selection is an anatomical inference, not proof of a unique optimal exercise or dose.
- **Triceps:** Two overhead-extension sets plus 12 pressing sets (eight fractional sets), with extra jerk work uncounted. The overhead position retains direct long-head work.
- **Quads:** Four squat and four leg-extension sets. Extensions complement squat training, including rectus femoris; Olympic work is additional but has no numerical hypertrophy credit.
- **Glutes:** Four full-depth squat sets plus Olympic work; zero isolation as requested. This is an explicit goal constraint, not a claim of maximum glute growth.
- **Hamstrings:** Six seated-leg-curl sets, three per exposure. Hip-flexed knee flexion trains the biarticular hamstrings at longer lengths and also trains the short biceps-femoris head. No extra failure hinge is added ahead of Olympic recovery.
- **Calves (gastrocnemius + soleus):** Six knee-extended calf-raise sets, three per exposure. Standing work covers gastrocnemius and soleus; adding seated work is not necessary just to include soleus.
- **Gastrocnemius (calf subset):** Six standing/knee-extended sets (the same calf sets). A seated fallback is not counted as equivalent gastrocnemius work.
- **Soleus (calf subset):** The same six calf sets also train soleus; they are not six additional exercises or sets.
- **Rectus abdominis:** Four controlled crunch sets plus unconverted bracing. Direct trunk flexion is retained instead of treating every squat as an abdominal failure set.
- **Adductors:** Zero isolation. Full-depth squats provide loaded adductor work; depth-specific longitudinal evidence supports this coverage. An exact set-equivalence or maximal-growth claim is not assigned.
- **Spinal erectors:** Zero isolation. Squats, pulls and Olympic lifts load spinal extension/bracing; more failure hinges add fatigue competing with the first priority. Supported rows avoid duplicating this burden.
- **Wrist flexors:** Four supported wrist-curl sets, two per exposure, after grip-dependent lifting. Direct wrist motion fills a gap that holding a bar does not reliably fill. The exact dose is a conservative regional inference.
- **Wrist extensors:** Four supported wrist-extension sets, two per exposure, after grip-dependent lifting. Pulling is not counted as equivalent extensor training; progress light loads with the same strict endpoint.
- **Finger flexors / grip:** Zero separate gripper sets: unstrapped Olympic lifts and pulling provide grip practice. Straps can reduce gripping in rows/pulldowns/shrugs, so that work has no fixed set credit. Wrist curls do not automatically count as finger-flexor sets.
- **Forearm pronators / supinators:** Zero dedicated pronation/supination sets. Curling and grip stabilization provide some exposure; dedicated maximal development is not claimed. The additional time is allocated to wrist flexion/extension and the stated physique priorities.
- **Obliques / deep abdominal wall:** Zero isolation. Squat/catch bracing and unilateral positions provide stabilization; this is not credited as failure-set hypertrophy. Additional loaded side bends/rotation are lower priority under the present time and fatigue constraints.
- **Hip abductors / external rotators:** Zero isolation, respecting the no-glute-isolation constraint. Squat/catch stabilization and eligible athletics provide task-specific work, not proof of maximum regional growth.
- **Hip flexors:** Zero loaded isolation. Dynamic preparation and eligible running provide movement exposure. No demonstrated goal-specific limitation justifies another failure block.
- **Tibialis anterior / dorsiflexors:** Zero loaded isolation. Ankle preparation, walking and eligible running provide activity; these are not hypertrophy sets. No dorsiflexor-specific size goal or documented deficit was supplied.
- **Foot intrinsics / ankle stabilizers:** Zero isolation. Calf work, receiving positions and eligible running/jumping load the foot/ankle. A specialized foot-strength dose is not assumed necessary without a relevant deficit.
- **Rotator cuff:** Zero failure isolation. Light external rotation is included in every lifting warm-up, with overhead stabilization in the lifts. Warm-ups count as preparation, not hypertrophy work; extra failure fatigue before catching is not prioritized.
- **Serratus anterior:** Zero isolation. Wall slides prepare upward rotation; overhead lifts require stabilization. This is a deliberate allocation to the main goals, not a maximal-serratus-growth claim.
- **Lower trapezius:** Zero isolation. Supported rows and overhead stabilization provide exposure, with wall-slide preparation. No numerical equivalence is claimed for stabilization.
- **Neck flexors / extensors:** Zero direct neck-failure sets. Traps and bracing are not treated as a neck-growth substitute, but a neck-size/contact-sport goal was not specified and another failure block has poor priority here.

The table does not turn warm-ups, ordinary walking or stabilization into failure-set credit. Grip is explicitly separate from wrist flexion/extension. Straps can reduce gripping in shrugs and pulls; no full or half-set forearm credit is assigned to those exercises, with or without straps. Keep setup/strap use consistent when comparing loads and reps. The new direct forearm rows are independent of that uncertain indirect exposure.

Every retained conventional exercise starts at one set per exposure. Stage 2 uses the source entry progression. Stage 3 uses the former established 56-set allocation plus one set of each new regional exercise per B/D exposure. Stage 4 applies the complete target. These stages are deliberate programming judgments; they are not a research-validated ramp. Advancing requires completed work, a green weekly review, normal next-session follow-ups and an eligible next phase/week. A normal week-1 start therefore commonly follows stages 1, 2, 3, 3, 4 in weeks 1–5. Good historical recovery does not automatically skip observations.

After the full allocation, two complete stable green weeks gate discretionary additions. Every existing exercise, including the three new rows, supports a controlled one-set addition or reduction. Two weeks mainly assess tolerance; physique evaluation needs longer trends. A fall of >20% in comparable first-set reps twice, deterioration in receiving positions, or impaired next Olympic sessions triggers review earlier. Progress does not prove that a dose is maximal. One change at a time remains the adjustment method.

Realization retains 14 Olympic sets because pulls are absent. Week 11 D reduces to one conventional set per row, giving 57 conventional weekly sets before other reductions. Week 12 retains four Olympic failure sets and two bench sets, without forearm/accessory work. Week 13 has no Olympic sets and 34 conventional sets (one per row/exposure). Readiness, local symptoms, alcohol/sport events, missing barbell protection and individualized restrictions take precedence.

The app displays the full time cost and Planner receives it unchanged. [Superset research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39903375/) supports time savings and similar average adaptations in the studied protocols, with higher internal load/RPE. It does not establish no performance sacrifice from densely pairing this entire long, failure-based Olympic program. This release therefore does not hide rests inside unimplemented supersets or falsify shorter durations. Choosing a guaranteed shorter visit AND claiming no sacrifice of any outcome is not supported by the available evidence. The full allocation remains a testable recommendation with explicit costs, not a claim that every constraint can be simultaneously maximized.
